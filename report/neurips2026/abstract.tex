Hardware-enabled monitoring of GPU workloads underpins many proposals for AI compute governance, but if developers can defeat monitoring mechanisms, such schemes are unworkable. We evaluate the adversarial robustness of GPU workload classification using only zero-overhead NVML telemetry: content-agnostic signals that observe physical effects of computation without slowing execution or accessing model weights, training data, or hyperparameters. Across 6 rounds of monitor-evader iteration, we evaluate 22 evasion strategy families on 9 GPU models spanning 4 architecture generations. We develop a classifier that achieves 98.7\% binary accuracy at identifying training workloads across the whole corpus, and 12--91\% accuracy against the most challenging unexpected workloads even when they are adversarially disguised.
